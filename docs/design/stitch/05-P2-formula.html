Derive the expectation value of the Hamiltonian for the trial wavefunction of a quantum harmonic oscillator.

Consider a trial wavefunction of the form:

$$ \psi(x, \alpha) = \left(\frac{2\alpha}{\pi}\right)^{1/4} e^{-\alpha x^2} $$

The Hamiltonian for the one-dimensional quantum harmonic oscillator is given by:

$$ \hat{H} = -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{d^2}{dx^2} + \frac{1}{2}m\omega^2 x^2 $$

Evaluate the integral for the expectation value $ \langle \hat{H} \rangle = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \psi^*(x, \alpha) \hat{H} \psi(x, \alpha) dx $ and find the value of $\alpha$ that minimizes this expectation value.

Student Response

The kinetic energy expectation value is calculated as:
$$ \langle T \rangle = \frac{\hbar^2 \alpha}{m} $$
The potential energy is:
$$ \langle V \rangle = \frac{m\omega^2}{8\alpha} $$
Total energy $E(\alpha) = \frac{\hbar^2 \alpha}{m} + \frac{m\omega^2}{8\alpha}$. Minimizing w.r.t $\alpha$:
$\frac{dE}{d\alpha} = \frac{\hbar^2}{m} - \frac{m\omega^2}{8\alpha^2} = 0 \implies \alpha = \frac{m\omega}{2\sqrt{2}\hbar}$
CORRECTION NOTED edit

Incorrect potential energy integral. Should be $\frac{m\omega^2}{8\alpha} \rightarrow \frac{m\omega^2}{8\alpha} $ is wrong, it is $\frac{m\omega^2}{8\alpha}$ ? No, $\langle x^2 \rangle = \frac{1}{4\alpha}$. So $\langle V \rangle = \frac{m\omega^2}{8\alpha}$. Minimization yields $\alpha = \frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}$.
